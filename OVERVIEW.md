# Diorama — Overview

An iOS app that shows a real city as a tiny tabletop model. You pick a city, put the phone in a head-mounted viewer, and see the city from above in stereo 3D. You stand still at one spot above the model; when you turn your head you look around it, and the city stays put.

## Product

- **Pick a city** (portrait, touch). Search box plus a curated list of cities that have Apple 3D coverage, plus recent cities. "Current location" opens where you stand in **live mode** (`?live=1` on the preview and Viewer routes): the city follows your GPS as you walk or ride, foreground only, and the Viewer faces true north so the model lines up with the real world; reopened from Recent it's a fixed place.
- **Preview** (portrait). A single-lens 3D map of the city slowly orbiting, with an **Enter Diorama** button. A glass map button top right (like Apple Maps') opens a native menu of **map styles** (T64): Satellite (photoreal Flyover, the default), Satellite with labels, or Standard (Apple Maps' drawn map with its 3D building models, light or dark with the phone). Below them a **Traffic** switch (T63, off by default) adds Apple Maps' live traffic on the roads; imagery alone can't show it, so while it's on Satellite is drawn as Satellite with labels (the menu checks that style), and picking Satellite turns traffic off. The choices are remembered and used by the Viewer and the Settings preview too; switching crossfades in place.
- **Viewer** (full screen). Upright: one full-screen picture held like a window into the city; moving the phone turns the view and one finger drags to look around. Sideways: side-by-side stereo for a head-mounted viewer, after a 3-second "Put on your viewer" countdown. It switches live as the phone turns. Head motion is first person: you look around from a fixed spot, all the way up to the sky (see Motion below). There is no touch UI while it's worn; a double-tap on the headset recenters and a long press exits.
- **Settings**. Eye separation ("model size"), tracking sensitivity, how strong the miniature blur is, and Two-eye view in landscape (on by default; off makes sideways full-screen mono too).

"Worn like Google Glass" means the phone sits in a head mount (Cardboard-style or a clip-on viewer) in landscape. The app must work without touch once it's worn.

## Research findings (short)

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| 3D satellite imagery from Apple Maps? | Yes. `MKMapView` with `MKImageryMapConfiguration(elevationStyle: .realistic)` (or `MKHybridMapConfiguration`) shows photoreal 3D "Flyover" meshes in supported cities. Outside those cities it falls back to flat satellite imagery on terrain. Use `MKStandardMapConfiguration(elevationStyle: .realistic)` as a fallback style. Still the best configuration on iOS 27 (T34, next row). |
| Map styles (T64) | `mapStyle`: `satellite` = `MKImageryMapConfiguration(elevationStyle: .realistic)`; `hybrid` = `MKHybridMapConfiguration(elevationStyle: .realistic)`, street and district names, no place icons; `standard` = `MKStandardMapConfiguration(elevationStyle: .realistic, emphasisStyle: .default)` with Apple Maps' place icons. Measured on iOS 27 (Simulator): all three keep 3D terrain; Satellite and Satellite with labels draw the same Flyover mesh; Standard draws MapKit's own extruded building models and landmark icons (dense in Boston; in Dubai, which has no Flyover, only a few buildings such as Dubai Mall, the Burj Khalifa as a flat landmark icon), and draws in dark colors in dark mode. Pitch caps are identical in all three (full-screen map: 70° to 1.25 km, 60° to 2.5 km, 35° beyond; stereo eye map: 70° to 600 m, 60° to 1.25 km, 35° from 1.5 km). In stereo, street and district names come out the same in both eyes (≤0.4 pt apart vertically, at their own depth), but place names don't: each eye's map resolves label collisions on its own, and after looking around, 3 of 4 views showed a place name in one eye only (`emphasisStyle: .muted` or a short list of categories didn't fix it). So a stereo eye's standard map shows no place icons; upright and in the previews they stay. Switching live keeps the camera (0.3 pt after the new tiles stand it on their ground, both eyes alike) and, with no head tracking, holds a still of the old map until the new one has drawn, then crossfades (MapKit shows its empty grid meanwhile otherwise). |
| Live traffic (T63) | `showsTraffic` exists only on `MKHybridMapConfiguration` and `MKStandardMapConfiguration` (the imagery configuration has none; the iOS 27 SDK adds nothing), so with traffic on, `satellite` is drawn as `hybrid` (`mapStyleShown` in `@diorama/native`); Standard stays Standard, and place icons stay as the style has them. Measured on iOS 27 (Simulator, Midtown Manhattan and Boston, a Friday at 5 pm): the Flyover mesh is the same with traffic as without (hybrid draws the classic mesh); only slow traffic draws (orange and red lines, no green, no incident icons), sparse where Apple has little data. The lines lie flat on the streets and are hidden by buildings in front of them, like the ground itself (they never float): from Midtown's default 1.2 km only short pieces show between towers, from 2 to 3 km (Camera height 2–3×) whole avenues. In stereo the traffic lines match in both eyes (≤1 pt apart vertically, a few points of depth disparity). Street names mostly do too, but in 4 of 8 views one street name showed in one eye only (3 near the lens's edge, 1 mid-view; the same with traffic off, so it's the hybrid labels', not traffic's). The hybrid configuration has no `emphasisStyle` or other public way to thin its labels, so traffic stays on in stereo pending the headset check. Switching traffic keeps the camera (0 px shift upright and in both stereo eyes) and crossfades like a style change. |
| iOS 27's new Flyover (Gaussian splats)? | **Not available to apps yet** (T34, Sept 2026). Apple's iOS 27 Maps has a sharper Flyover built from new aerial imagery and AI in "select cities" (e.g. New York, Cupertino, London), rolling out city by city. The press reads it as Gaussian splatting; Apple hasn't said so. There's no developer API for it. The iOS 27 SDK's MapKit headers and Swift interfaces add only new point-of-interest categories: no new map configuration, no new elevation style (`MKMapConfiguration.ElevationStyle` is still `flat`/`realistic`), no quality, level-of-detail or anti-aliasing setting, and no "is 3D here?" query. The iOS 27 release notes have no MapKit section, and Apple's MapKit updates page has no 2026 entry. iOS 27's own map renderer does contain a Flyover splat pipeline, and Apple's tile service offers iOS 27 devices new Flyover tile sets that it doesn't offer iOS 26.5. Even so, in the iOS 27.0 Simulator an app's `MKMapView` never loads them with any public configuration (imagery, hybrid or standard with realistic elevation, or the old flyover map types) in New York, Cupertino or London. It loads the same classic Flyover mesh tiles as iOS 26.5 and draws the same way: same pitch caps, stereo vertical disparity ≤0.1 pt, Apple's logo and Legal in both eyes within 0.5 pt. So the app stays on the configuration above, and if MapKit starts drawing the new 3D by itself (on the phone, or in a later iOS), Diorama gets it with no change. Private MapKit switches exist, but the App Store forbids private APIs. The same holds for the flat hybrid map of "Choose on map" (T43). Sources: [Apple newsroom, WWDC26](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2026/06/apple-unveils-innovative-features-and-intelligence-experiences-across-services/), [iOS 27 release notes](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/ios-ipados-release-notes/ios-ipados-27-release-notes), [MapKit updates](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/updates/mapkit), [AppleInsider](https://appleinsider.com/articles/26/06/09/pretty-trees-and-local-lists-apple-maps-gets-a-big-upgrade-in-ios-27). |
| Camera control | `MKMapCamera(lookingAtCenter:fromDistance:pitch:heading:)`. Setting `mapView.camera` without animation every frame is supported. **There is no roll.** Fake head roll by rotating each eye's picture (part of its warp). Pitch is capped by MapKit, and the cap depends on how far out the camera is; MapKit never looks up to the horizon (see Motion). |
| Stereo from MapKit? | **No.** No stereo or multi-view API. Use **two `MKMapView`s**, one per eye. MapKit anchors each camera to the surface (rooftops included) under its look-at point, so shifting each eye's center sideways misaligns the eyes vertically. Instead, both cameras look at the same point (the model center, or with head tracking the aim point) from eye positions ±baseline/2 apart (toe-in), and each picture is warped by a homography back to an ideal parallel eye (see `StereoGeometry.swift`). |
| Why it looks tiny | This is the *hyperstereo* effect. When the baseline between the two eyes is much wider than human eyes (~6.4 cm), your brain reads the scene as a small model. Baseline ≈ altitude / 50 (was / 30; lowered in T22 so a Cardboard-style viewer fuses comfortably), adjustable in Settings. Add a tilt-shift style blur (sharp band in the middle, blurred above and below) and a slight saturation boost to sell the effect. |
| Motion | `CMMotionManager.deviceMotion` (100 Hz) with reference frame `.xArbitraryCorrectedZVertical` (`.xTrueNorthZVertical` in live mode, T59; see True north below). Read it **in Swift** on a `CADisplayLink` and set both cameras in the same frame. Do not send motion over the JS bridge. **First person** (T23, `FirstPersonCamera.swift`): the head stays at a fixed *vantage point*, the starting camera's own eye position (from its center, altitude as camera-to-center distance, pitch and heading). Head yaw and pitch turn the *gaze* from there; the camera looks at the *aim point*, where the gaze meets the ground. So the city stays world-fixed and the view pans across it, instead of the camera circling the model center. Camera turn = head turn × window FOV rendered ÷ FOV seen through the lens (about 0.61 for the default 35 mm round window 40 mm behind the lens; see `ViewerProfile.lookGain`), times the sensitivity setting, so a head turn of N° moves the city N° through the lens. **Looking up has no stop** (T31): the gaze runs from straight down to straight up, and heading turns round and round. The gain holds exactly for 30° of nod either way from where you recentered, then bends smoothly so that nodding 90° up looks straight up and 90° down straight down (at 0.61 alone no neck could reach the sky). MapKit's camera follows the gaze only as far up as MapKit draws from the vantage point (it asks MapKit up front); each eye's warp turns the picture the rest of the way, which is exact at every depth because first-person look is a turn in place. **MapKit's limit, honestly:** MapKit caps pitch by distance (measured on the stereo eye's map, iOS 26 and 27 alike: 70° out to about 700 m, 60° at 0.9–1.25 km, 35° from 1.5 km), draws only a 30°-tall slice, and draws no sky or haze of its own: its farthest city is sharp right to the picture's top edge, which from the vantage point stays about 12° (0.5× Camera height), 15° (1×) or 40° (2×) below the horizon. So when you look higher, the band between the top of the real city and the horizon is synthesized haze (`SkyBackdrop.swift`), not distant city: the city fades into it over the top 4.5° of MapKit's picture, and above the horizon the haze clears to blue overhead. The sky is identical in both eyes up to their zero-parallax slide, which puts it at infinity. From far out MapKit won't draw even a city's own pitch, so while tracking you start from the steepest pitch it draws there (T30), looking more straight down with the model center still in the middle. Apple's logo and Legal link ride the circle's lower edge as the city slides down (the map's margins grow evenly), until the city fills less than about half the circle; MapKit hides its logo in a smaller box, so past that they slide out with the city. Roll: in stereo any head roll is cancelled (a round eye turned is the same circle, and its map covers the diameter); mono cancels up to 20°. Recenter re-zeroes the head, not the vantage point; new camera props mean a new vantage point. The one-euro filter lets go as soon as the head moves (about 3 ms behind at 100°/s). Known limit: MapKit stands every camera on the ground height under its look-at point, so the vantage point bobs by about 1% of its height over dense city. **Head position** (T46, `HeadPosition.swift`, `headPosition` prop): lean in to get closer, like leaning over a real tabletop model. CoreMotion still does rotation (100 Hz, lowest latency); ARKit world tracking (`ARWorldTrackingConfiguration`, rear camera, no view, no planes, depth or meshing; the smallest camera format at the highest frame rate the thermal budget allows, never 4K) adds only where the head is. The eyes' move since recenter (the camera's pose moved to a point 6 cm in front of the screen, so turning your head doesn't read as a lean), in a level frame turned to where you faced at recenter, moves the vantage point: down sinks you toward the city, forward and sideways move you over it (motion parallax), along the base camera's right and forward. Scale: real meters × stereo baseline ÷ 0.064 m (so one eye spacing of head move is one baseline: the model stays one size; 375× from 1.2 km) × `leanGain`; the baseline stays fixed while you lean. Limits reuse the pinch's (T37) rubber band: the distance to the ground ahead along the resting gaze stays within 300 m–5 km (widened to take in where you stand), leaning up stops where MapKit stops drawing the middle of the view (the ~3 km haze stop), out over the city no farther than the camera's distance; MapKit's pitch cap is asked by height step as for a pinch. It adds to pinch zoom and the live-follow glide. ARKit's pose is ~30 ms old when used, so CoreMotion's acceleration carries it on to now (restarting from each new ARKit pose, so it never drifts), then a one-euro filter (1 Hz at rest, +40 Hz per m/s) smooths it. States (`onHeadPositionState`: off, starting, tracking, limited): when ARKit loses track the position holds 0.5 s, then eases back to rotation only over 1.5 s; on its return it picks up from there (a short loss glides back to the true position); recenter and a turn of the phone re-zero it over 0.35 s. Never jumps. It never asks for the camera itself (`requestCameraAccess()`), and pauses when the phone is critically hot. **Up and down off** (T61, `leanVertical` prop, Settings' "Move up and down", on by default): only the level part of the move counts (ARKit's world is gravity-aligned, so its y is dropped before smoothing, and the jump check measures the level part too), so standing up, sitting down or bobbing keeps your height while leaning forward and sideways still moves you, upright and in stereo. Switched off mid-lean, the height eases back over 0.5 s (it moves into the hand-off's carry); switched on, up and down count from where the head is then. The Simulator has no ARKit: with debugLook, `setDebugLean` (dev map `lean=`) feeds a fake move through the same smoothing, scale and limits. **True north** (T59, `trueNorth` prop, on only in live mode): CoreMotion runs in `.xTrueNorthZVertical`, but the look's yaw adds up the gyro alone (`HeadPose.yawRate`, within 0.1° of the attitude's yaw in a simulated 6 s of turning), so the compass settling or correcting itself never swims the view. While the compass is trusted (iOS 27's `CMDeviceMotion.headingAccuracy`, CoreLocation's `CLHeading.headingAccuracy` before; trusted at ≤20°, lost past 30°) the base camera turns so the city's straight ahead faces the real heading you face; the place's `heading` is then ignored. The turn eases on a critically damped spring (1.2 s, 0.3 s under Reduce Motion), so the vantage point swings round the model center (you) and never jumps; it keeps following the compass past a 0.5° deadband (which also takes out gyro drift) and stays while tracking pauses. The lens gain still applies, so the model matches the world exactly straight ahead and a little less the farther you turn. Recenter keeps north: it levels pitch and roll and brings what you face now to straight ahead, the city turning with it (the look and the base camera ease together); the Viewer recenters as its countdown ends, so whatever you face when the headset goes on lines up. A poor compass holds the city where it is (`onCompassState` `calibrating`; the Viewer's HUD asks for a figure 8 after 1.5 s); after 10 s of it (a headset's magnet) the sensors go back to `.xArbitraryCorrectedZVertical` for the session (`unavailable`), keeping any alignment, without a jump. iOS 27's `CMMotionManager.deviceMotionBody` (a `UIView` as `CMBodyIdentifiable`) could replace the hand-written `ScreenAxes`, but its header documents no axes and the Simulator has no motion to compare, so `ScreenAxes` stays until a device check. |
| Can react-native-maps do this? | Not well. It doesn't give per-frame control of two synced realistic-elevation cameras. Use a small custom **Expo Module** in Swift. |
| City search | `MKLocalSearchCompleter` + `MKLocalSearch`, run natively. No API key or cost. Wrap them with TanStack Query in TS. |
| Simulator | Flyover renders in the Simulator, but there is no gyro. Motion needs a real iPhone. Build a "fake motion" debug mode (drag to look) for the Simulator. |

## Architecture

```
React Native (TypeScript, Expo dev client, New Architecture, iOS 16+)
├── expo-router (native stack)   — file-based routing, typed routes, real swipe-back + large titles
├── @tanstack/react-query        — every async/native call (search, flyover availability)
├── zustand (+ persist/MMKV)     — settings + recents (no prop drilling)
├── expo-symbols, expo-haptics, expo-blur, expo-keep-awake, expo-screen-orientation
└── modules/diorama-native (Expo Module, Swift)   ← the only Swift in the app
    ├── DioramaMapView      — native view: 1 or 2 MKMapViews, camera, tilt-shift overlay
    ├── HeadTracker         — CMMotionManager + CADisplayLink → head look, true north in live mode (all native)
    ├── HeadPosition        — ARKit world tracking + accelerometer → where the head is (lean in)
    ├── FirstPersonCamera   — head look → gaze from a fixed vantage point → camera (pure math)
    ├── SkyBackdrop         — haze and sky behind each eye, past what MapKit draws
    └── DioramaSearch       — async functions: autocomplete(query), resolve(completionId)
```

**Rule of thumb:** anything that runs every frame lives in Swift. Anything the user sees and edits (screens, layout, copy, settings, flow) lives in React. The Swift surface stays small and has a typed TS wrapper, so the user (who knows React, not Swift) can change behavior through props.

### Native view props (the TS contract)

```ts
type DioramaMapViewProps = {
  center: { latitude: number; longitude: number };
  altitude: number;          // meters
  pitch: number;             // degrees
  heading: number;           // degrees
  mode: 'mono' | 'stereo';
  eyeSeparation: number;     // multiplier on altitude-derived baseline
  headTracking: boolean;
  debugLook?: boolean;       // Simulator: drag to look instead of gyro
  trackingSensitivity: number;
  headPosition?: boolean;    // lean in to get closer (ARKit; needs headTracking and camera access). Default false
  leanGain?: number;         // × the true-to-scale lean (baseline ÷ 6.4 cm). Default 1
  leanVertical?: boolean;    // false: leaning up and down (standing, sitting) leaves your height alone. Default true
  onHeadPositionState?: (e: { state: 'off' | 'starting' | 'tracking' | 'limited' }) => void;
  trueNorth?: boolean;       // live mode: face the real compass heading (needs headTracking). Default false
  onCompassState?: (e: { state: 'good' | 'calibrating' | 'unavailable' }) => void;
  miniatureIntensity: number; // 0..1 tilt-shift + saturation
  mapStyle?: 'satellite' | 'hybrid' | 'standard'; // the map's look (T64); changes in place. Default satellite
  showsTraffic?: boolean;    // Apple Maps' live traffic (T63); satellite is drawn as hybrid while on. Default false
  orbit: boolean;            // slow auto-rotate (preview screen)
  showsUserLocation?: boolean; // Apple's blue dot in every eye (live mode)
  onReady?: (e: { coverage: 'yes' | 'no' | 'unknown' }) => void; // 3D buildings here? From hand-checked lists
};
// ref methods: recenter(), setDebugLook(dx, dy), beginZoom(), setZoom(scale), endZoom(), resetZoom() (portrait pinch),
//   followTo(latitude, longitude) (live mode: the model center glides to a new GPS fix natively; not a new place,
//   so no loading cover or onReady, and look, zoom, orbit and eye separation stay; JS sends at most ~1 fix a second),
//   setDebugLean(right, up, forward, tracking?) (Simulator stand-in for head position, meters)
// module functions: getCameraAccess(), requestCameraAccess() → 'granted' | 'denied' | 'undetermined' | 'unsupported'
//   (head position never asks for the camera itself; ask upright, before the headset goes on)
```

## Code rules

- TypeScript `strict`. No `any`.
- Keep components small: one screen is a thin route component plus a few child components. Hooks own the logic.
- **No prop drilling.** Shared state goes in zustand stores. Server/native async goes through TanStack Query hooks (`useCitySearch`, `useCity`).
- Routing only through expo-router (`useRouter`, `useLocalSearchParams`, `<Link>`, typed routes). Route files in `app/` stay thin and render a screen from `src/screens/`. No ad-hoc screen state.
- Keep Swift minimal, commented for a React developer, and exposed only through typed props, events, and functions.

## "Made by Apple" bar

- SF Pro via the system font. SF Symbols via `expo-symbols`. System colors that follow light/dark mode. Support Dynamic Type.
- Native stack navigation (expo-router `Stack`): real large titles, header search bar, edge swipe-back. Inset-grouped lists, materials (blur) in place of flat fills, haptics on meaningful actions.
- Motion: spring animations (Reanimated), no linear fades. Respect Reduce Motion.
- Copy: short, sentence case, no exclamation marks.
- Viewer: no chrome over the eyes. A brief HUD fades in on recenter, then fades out. The only control is a small glass close button in the black margin outside the eye windows (invisible through the lenses); holding anywhere for a second also exits.

## Known risks

1. **Two MKMapViews at 60 fps** is GPU/thermal heavy. Mitigations: cap the frame rate at 30–60, watch `ProcessInfo.thermalState` and drop to mono, pause the orbit.
2. **Tile streaming mismatch.** Each eye streams its own tiles, so one eye can briefly show different detail than the other. Keep the baseline small relative to the view, and wait for both views to finish rendering before showing the viewer.
3. **Orientation.** app.json must say `orientation: "default"`. If it says `portrait`, iOS can never rotate to landscape. Portrait is locked at runtime through native stack screen options, and only the Viewer route (a full-screen modal) turns: every way but upside down (`orientation: 'default'`), and how the phone is held picks its mode.
4. **No roll in MKMapCamera.** Roll is faked by rotating each eye's picture. The round stereo eyes need no extra overscan for it (their maps cover the circle's diameter, and a circle turned is the same circle), so roll is free there; the full-screen mono picture overscans to hide the corners up to 20° of roll.
5. **ARKit plus two maps.** Head position runs the camera and ARKit's tracking at up to 60 frames a second on top of two MKMapViews, which adds heat and battery drain. Mitigations: the smallest camera format and no depth, planes or meshing; the camera drops to a 30 fps format (if the phone has one) when the budget drops to 30, and ARKit stops at `critical` (rotation only). Measure 10 minutes in the headset on the real phone (T15). iOS also shows its green camera-in-use dot while it runs.
6. **Headset optics.** Cardboard lenses need barrel distortion correction. That's in the backlog as an optional setting; glasses-style viewers don't need it.
