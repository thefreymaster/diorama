# Mini Cities: app icon concepts (T62)

Five candidates for a new app icon. Nothing here is wired into the app yet: the shipping icon is still `assets/expo.icon` (T17).

Open **`contact-sheet.png`** first. It shows all five and the current icon at 1024, 180, 60 and 29 px, in light, dark, tinted and clear looks on light and dark backgrounds, in a mock home-screen row between placeholder icons, and side by side.

Each folder has:

- an Icon Composer bundle (`*.icon`) in the same format as `assets/expo.icon`, with separate layers so iOS 26's Liquid Glass can light and separate them
- `light.png`, `dark.png` and `tinted.png`, each 1024 × 1024, exported by Icon Composer's own renderer (`ictool` in Xcode 27). Tinted uses an amber tint (hue 0.1, strength 0.75).

| # | Concept | The idea | Pitch |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | [Glass dome](01-glass-dome/) | A tiny city in a snow globe on a wooden base. The globe is a real Liquid Glass layer, so it catches light on the Home Screen. In dark mode the glass goes smoky and the city stays in colour. | A whole city you can hold: the "tiny world" idea in one round shape. |
| 2 | [Tilt-shift street](02-tilt-shift/) | A row of toy-coloured buildings, sharp in the middle band and blurred above (far towers) and below (street and tree tops), like the app's miniature effect. At night (dark mode) the sky turns deep blue. | The app's own miniature look: a real street that looks like a model. |
| 3 | [Two lenses](03-two-lenses/) | Two round eye windows on graphite, each showing the same little city and a far peak. The buildings shift a few points between the eyes, as in stereo. The lens rims are Liquid Glass. | What you see in the headset: two eyes, one little city. |
| 4 | [Museum plinth](04-museum-plinth/) | A mountain peak, pines and two buildings on a model board, displayed on a white museum plinth. In dark mode the plinth turns graphite. | Cities and national parks as museum models. |
| 5 | [Pop-up map](05-popup-map/) | A folded paper map lying open, with a mountain, a tower, a house and a pine standing up from it like a pop-up book. In dark mode the paper turns graphite. | A map that stands up off the page. |

My read from the contact sheet:

- **Two lenses** stands out most at 60 and 29 px, and it's the only one that says "headset".
- **Glass dome** has the clearest single silhouette.
- **Museum plinth** and **Pop-up map** are the only ones that show national parks as well as cities.
- **Tilt-shift street** is the busiest. Its blur depends on the whole picture, so at 29 px it reads as a colourful strip. It's also the only one whose blurred layers are PNGs rather than vector art.

All five avoid text and photos of real places and sit on the standard 1024 canvas. Each keeps one main subject inside the icon grid's safe area; only the tilt-shift scene runs to the edges.

## Switching to one

1. Replace the contents of `assets/expo.icon/` with the chosen bundle's contents (`icon.json` and `Assets/`). `app.json` already points at `./assets/expo.icon`, so it doesn't change.
2. Update the flat fallback `assets/images/icon.png`. It must be 1024 × 1024 and full-bleed, with no transparency. Export it from Icon Composer, or flatten `light.png` onto the background colour.
3. Run `npx expo prebuild -p ios`, bump the build number and archive.

## Rebuilding the art

Everything is generated by small scripts in `tools/`. They use only tools already on the Mac: Python 3, `xcrun swift`, and Icon Composer's `ictool`. No npm packages are involved.

```sh
python3 design/icons/tools/build.py     # writes the .icon bundles (SVG layers; tilt-shift blurs via Core Image)
design/icons/tools/render.sh            # light/dark/tinted PNGs + contact-sheet.png via ictool
```

Each concept is one Python file (`glass_dome.py`, `tilt_shift.py`, `two_lenses.py`, `museum_plinth.py`, `popup_map.py`). You can also open any `.icon` in Icon Composer and fine-tune the glass, shadows or colours there. If you do, don't re-run `build.py` on that concept, because it rewrites the bundle.

A few Icon Composer behaviours the bundles rely on:

- In dark mode, a layer drawn only in white takes the background colour. The snow in the globe has a dark-mode fill so it stays white.
- A background with its own dark look must list both looks under `fill-specializations`. A plain `fill` alone is ignored in dark mode.
- The `tinted` opacity specializations fade backdrops (the lens sky, the map paper, the plinth, the blurred street) so the subject stays brightest in the tinted and clear looks.
